Method

What the desk reads, how it turns those numbers into a price, how it grades confidence, and what it refuses to do.

1. Capture

Each sport's fixtures come from its governing body, league, tour or federation — never from an odds aggregator. The page is read with the agent fetch tool and the numbers are transcribed into data/cadence/fixtures.json with the source key, the URL and the fetch timestamp. Timestamps are stored exactly as the publisher prints them; where the basis is not stated (for example a URC “local” time) the desk stores the string and does not convert it.

3. Grading

high needs P ≥ 70% and evidence tier A (a full-season table or ranking) or tier B (4–9 matches this season). Where only 1–3 matches exist, a natural money-line selection can still be graded high because the probability is what the official numbers imply, but an alternate band line on the same thin sample is capped at medium. Every cap is printed on the row. medium is 60–70%.

4. The band

Fair American odds between −250 and +10000. −250 is 71.43%, so this band and P ≥ 70% overlap only in 70.00–71.43%. The desk publishes the fair price of every selection, marks it in or out of band, and quotes an alternate line at the band ceiling where the ladder allows. See the band board.

5. What the desk will not do

  • Publish a price for a fixture where an official source does not name both participants.
  • Use an observed bookmaker price as an input. The NHL feed's moneylines are shown as a comparison and labelled as such.
  • Assert an injury outside the NBA's own game-day report.
  • Convert a time when the publisher has not stated its basis.
  • Write a prediction for a selection below medium confidence.
  • Use the words “model” or “verified” in reader-facing prose.
Every published row links to the official governing-body page it came from. Values that an official source did not publish are left blank and listed on Flagged for review. Prices are the desk's own fair lines, never observed bookmaker quotes unless a row says so.
