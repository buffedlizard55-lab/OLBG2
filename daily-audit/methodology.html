Methodology

This layer publishes a dated snapshot: officially verified fixtures, fair prices this layer originates itself, a confidence label per selection, and written predictions for the five highest-confidence picks. It is not a live feed and it is not betting advice.

1. Fixtures

A fixture is published only when an official governing-body page prints the pairing. The official page is linked on the row and its rendered text is stored verbatim under data/daily-audit/capture/. Discovery comes from OLBG, but OLBG is never used as the authority for a pairing, a date or a price: OLBG publishes tip counts, not odds. Dates and times are printed as the official source printed them; nothing is converted between timezones.

2. Inputs

The only strength inputs are the official ATP seeding, the entry status (seeded, unseeded, qualifier) and the sets each player actually won and lost in the tournament according to the official draw. A player who entered on a bye has a zero form input, and that gap is disclosed rather than filled. Cricket has no input at all in this session because the ICC rankings table rendered empty, so cricket carries no price.

3. The price model

  • rating = 1500 + 25 × (9 − seed) for a seeded player
  • rating = 1480 unseeded, 1455 for a qualifier
  • rating += 6 × (sets won − sets lost) from verified scorelines only
  • p(A wins) = 1 ÷ (1 + 10^(−(rating_A − rating_B) ÷ 200))
  • games margin ~ Normal(8.0 × (p − 0.5), 4.5) — drives every handicap line
  • total games ~ Normal(22.0, 4.2) — drives every total line
  • fair decimal = 1 ÷ p, rounded from a probability rounded to four decimals first, so a reader can reproduce every printed price

These constants are this layer's assumptions. No governing body publishes them. They are stated here in full so anyone can re-run python3 scripts/daily_audit_model.py with different values and see how far the prices move.

4. Confidence and the price window

high means model probability ≥ 0.7000; medium 0.6000–0.6999; low below 0.6000. The brief's price condition — a fair American price between −250 and +10000 — is reported separately in a −250 window column: −250 equals a probability of 0.714286, so the condition holds only for probabilities between 0.7000 and 0.714286. That 1.43-point window is stepped over by half-point lines at the margin and total deviations used here, so this layer publishes zero in-window picks and says so on every page rather than adjusting a line until one appears (DL-05).

5. Predictions

A written prediction exists only for a published pick. Each is at least 30 words, names the selection, states the tournament form and a recent completed scoreline, and lists each fact with the official URL it came from. No paragraph names a player, a score or an opponent that is not in the fixture's fact list, and no paragraph implies a bookmaker price.

6. Reproducing the layer

python3 scripts/daily_audit_model.py && python3 scripts/daily_audit_build.py

The model reads data/daily-audit/fixtures.json and data/daily-audit/inputs.json, writes data/daily-audit/lines.json, and the builder renders this site. The build is deterministic: no clock, no network, no randomness. python3 -m unittest tests.test_daily_audit checks the rendered pages, not only the JSON.

7. Limits stated up front

  • Two sports have rows here. Fifteen of the seventeen eligible sports do not, and each reason is listed on the landing page and on Flagged for Review.
  • The sample is one day of one sport's quarter-finals. Eight tennis fixtures cannot support any claim about the model's long-run accuracy.
  • Seeding is a proxy for strength, not a measurement of it. A bye inflates the uncertainty and the model does not add a variance term for rust.
  • Retirement, walkover and in-play states are not modelled. A walkover is recorded as a statement about the draw, never as a completed score.
