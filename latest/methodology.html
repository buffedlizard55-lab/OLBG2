Method

Four pricing families and one confidence rule, all stated so a reader can reproduce a price. No bookmaker price is read anywhere in this desk.

Where the inputs come from

Each fixture carries a list of claims, and each claim carries the page it was read from and the time it was read. The fixture records were captured at 17:35Z on 24 October 2026 and are republished here unchanged with that capture time; the Formula 1 race was captured first hand after qualifying.

How a price is made

FamilySportsWhat it does
Poisson scoringIce Hockey, BaseballExpected goals or runs come from each side's scoring and conceding rates; the score grid gives the winner, handicap and total chances.
Normal marginRugby Union, Rugby League, Gaelic Sports, Basketball Mean margin from points for and against with a stated home edge, converted to a chance with a stated spread.
Evidence ladderVolleyball, Handball, Cricket, TennisEach side is placed on a rung from the sourced facts on its record; one rung is worth ten probability points from 50%.
Grid curveMotor RacingChance falls geometrically down the published starting grid, with the curve fixed so pole is worth a stated 40% share.

Prices are the reciprocal of the chance, rounded to two decimals, and the American figure is derived from that same decimal so the two always agree.

Confidence

GradeRule
High70% chance or better and the price inside −250…+10000. A side rated above 71.43% cannot satisfy both, so it is moved to a handicap or total rung.
Medium60% chance or better.
LowEverything else. Published with the label on it, never presented as a strong call.

What is never done

  • A value that is not on an official page stays blank, and the blank is published with its reason.
  • No injury, line-up or travel claim is made unless the official record for that fixture carries it.
  • No market is priced where both sides' inputs are missing.
  • Displayed times are never converted between time zones.
