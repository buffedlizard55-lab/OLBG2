Method

How a match gets from an OLBG listing to a price, a confidence label and — sometimes — a written prediction. Every step is executed by a script in scripts/, and the pages are rendered from the JSON those scripts write.

1 · Scope

Fifteen sports, exactly as the brief lists them: Golf, Volleyball, Rugby Union, Cricket, Snooker, Darts, Baseball, Gaelic Sports, Greyhounds, Tennis, Motor Racing, Handball, Ice Hockey, Basketball and Rugby League. Gaelic football and hurling sit under Gaelic Sports. Aussie Rules, esports, football, horse racing, American football, boxing and MMA are out of scope and are not published here.

2 · Where the fixture list comes from

The stored OLBG listing pages are re-parsed by scripts/unified_inventory.py. Each tip object in the page payload contributes an event name, an OLBG event id, a start timestamp and a market name. Events are de-duplicated on sport plus event id, and an event is marked upcoming only when its recorded start is later than the capture clock. The parser re-hashes every stored body against the manifest and refuses a file whose hash has moved.

3 · What proves a fixture

Preference order, strongest first: a machine-readable body from the league itself (MLB statsapi, NHL api-web, EuroLeague schedule XML), joined on the fixture id and checked on both sides; then the publishing session's captured reading of the competition's page, labelled as such. A fixture that neither family supports is not published.

4 · How a price is originated

Each sport has a strength input taken from the competition's own published numbers: league points or win percentages, goals or points scored and conceded, and a home term where the competition has one. Those become a win probability, a handicap distribution or a total expectation. The fair price is the inverse of the probability; the offered price applies a fixed margin. The basis column on Our prices prints the arithmetic so a reader can redo it.

5 · Confidence

High is a chance of 70% or more, medium is 60–70%, low is under 60%. One documented exception: an alternate band line on a thin sample is capped at medium even when its probability clears 70%. Those rows are marked band_line in the data, and the audit counts them separately (37 rows) rather than letting the cap go unrecorded.

6 · Which picks get written up

Only selections at medium confidence or better receive prose, one per fixture-market. Where a sport has fewer than five such selections it gets fewer than five write-ups: the desk does not promote a low-confidence row to meet a quota. The current shortfall is on the overview and in flag U-05.

7 · What the prose may contain

At least thirty words; a unique opening three words; the selection named in the text; only figures that appear in that fixture's stored facts, inputs or prices; and none of the words the brief bans from output. The last two rules are machine-checked by scripts/unified_audit.py (checks A3 and A8), not left to proofreading.

8 · Reproducing this site

python3 scripts/unified_inventory.py   # re-parse the stored OLBG bodies
python3 scripts/unified_audit.py       # re-derive every number and every check
python3 scripts/unified_build.py       # render these pages

The three scripts read only committed files and write deterministic output, so the validation workflow can prove the published pages match the published data.

What this desk is. A dated, source-linked research record of sport listed on OLBG, of our own originated prices, and of confidence-graded selections. It is not a live odds feed and it does not place bets.

Every price, probability and confidence label is our own estimate, derived from the official statistics named on the row. Values that could not be confirmed are left blank and listed on Flagged for review. Capture times and source files are printed on every table.
