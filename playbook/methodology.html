Method

How a price and a grade get made here.

Two inputs, never mixed silently, and a rubric written down before the picks were graded.

1. Where the matches come from

Every match is a row OLBG itself listed on one of its sixteen sport hubs, read first-hand on 24 October 2026 between 22:35 and 23:05 UTC. The hub in scope for each sport is listed on Sources. OLBG's hub is a tip feed, not a fixture list: it only shows an event once at least one tip has been placed on it. That is why Golf, Snooker, Motor Racing and Rugby League have no matches — those hubs returned "No tips found." or, for Rugby League, only 2027 outrights.

2. Where the prices come from

Tier A, 5 fixtures. The five NHL games on 25 October. Each club's goals for and goals against per game are read from the NHL's own season summary, regressed toward the league mean with fifteen prior games because ten games is a small sample, adjusted for home ice (and not adjusted for the neutral-site Heritage Classic), and run through a bivariate Poisson. That produces a win price, a puck line and two totals.

Tier B, 90 fixtures. Everything else. OLBG publishes the share of its own tips on the highlighted selection. That share is already normalised across the market's outcomes, so it is read as the market's implied chance for that selection. A share built on a handful of tips is not a probability, so it is pulled toward 50% with six pseudo-tips before it is priced. Only the market OLBG printed can be originated this way: a handicap or a total needs scoring data that this capture does not contain for these sports.

Both tiers then get the same margin: the fair decimal price from the chance, with 4% taken off the win part.

3. How confidence is graded

GradeRule
highA governing-body input behind the price, a chance of 65% or better, and a price inside −250 to +10000. All three.
mediumA price inside the band with a chance of 50% or better, or a chance of 70% or better whose price falls outside the band.
lowEverything else.

Why the rule is not simply "over 70%". A price of −250 is a 71.43% chance. A rule that calls anything above 70% high, combined with a rule that anything shorter than −250 is out of scope, can only ever be satisfied inside a 1.4-point window. On this slate 16 selections cleared 70%, 175 prices sat inside the band, and 3 did both. The grade therefore asks for evidence as well as probability, and the band is shown as its own column on every table so the shortfall is visible rather than hidden.

4. What a written preview has to contain

  • At least 30 words, one author, no repeated opening across previews.
  • The selection named in the heading, so the bet is never ambiguous.
  • Team or player, competition, the situation, and the numbers the case rests on.
  • A fact list under each preview, with a link to where each fact was read.
  • No internal jargon in the prose: the terms this desk refuses to publish are enforced by the verifier, which fails the build if one appears in a preview.

5. What is deliberately absent

OLBG serves no bookmaker price to this location, so no market price is quoted anywhere on the desk. No injury list is published against any fixture, because no governing-body injury report was read. No tennis tournament name is given, because no official draw page returned to this reader. All of that is on Flagged.

What this desk is. A read of the matches OLBG listed across fifteen sports on the evening of 24 October 2026, with a price this desk originated for each match it could price, a confidence grade, and a written preview where the evidence carried one. Every row links to the page it came from, and every fact in a preview links to the page it was read from.
